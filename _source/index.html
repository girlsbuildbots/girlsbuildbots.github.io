Girls Build Bots connects Girl Scout troops with FIRST robotics teams so Brownies, Juniors, and Cadettes can earn official STEM badges — from Robotics and Coding to Cybersecurity, Automotive, Space Science, and more — through hands-on workshops taught by students who build competition robots.

The project

Why this exists

Robotics outreach events are everywhere — but most of them aren't structured to let Girl Scouts earn their official badges. Troop leaders often don't have the technical background to run STEM badge work themselves, and robotics teams don't know what badge requirements look like. Girls end up doing great STEM activities that count for… nothing on their vest.

Girls Build Bots closes that gap with three things: a badge library covering the STEM badges at each level, ready-to-teach curriculum, slide decks, and mentor guides for the Robotics series, and a pairing hub where troops and teams find each other. It was created as a Girl Scout Gold Award project by Gabriella Angryk (Troop 383, Girl Scouts of Greater Atlanta) with Walton Robotics — and it's built so any troop and any team, anywhere, can use it.

For Girl Scout troops

Find a FIRST robotics team near you

  • FIRST Team & Event Search — the official locator. Search your ZIP code for FRC and FTC teams; team pages list contact info.
  • Ask your local high school — most FRC teams are school-based and reachable through the front office or an engineering teacher.
  • Attend a competition (free to watch!) and talk to teams at their pits — they love visitors.
  • In metro Atlanta? Walton Robotics (FRC 2974) helped build this program — use the pairing form below.
For robotics teams

Find Girl Scout troops to serve

  • Contact your local Girl Scout council's program staff — in metro Atlanta that's Girl Scouts of Greater Atlanta; elsewhere, use the council finder.
  • Councils can post your workshop in their event calendars and reach hundreds of troops at once.
  • Service Unit meetings (local troop leader gatherings) are a great place to pitch a workshop.
  • Everything you need to teach is free on this site: curriculum, slides, mentor guides, and surveys.

How a workshop works

Pick badges. Prep together. Build.

1

Troop leaders pick badges and review the official requirements in the free Girl Scout Volunteer Toolkit, then order badges from the council shop. Everything on this site aligns with badge themes — leaders verify completion against the official steps.

2

The robotics team preps with our materials — the Robotics series has full meeting plans, slide decks, mentor guides, and checklists; every other STEM badge page has a session idea and the best free teaching resources. Mentor training takes about an hour.

3

Scouts build, program, and present — capped with a real competition-robot demo and a showcase for families. Then leave a review on the badge page so the next team teaches it even better.
