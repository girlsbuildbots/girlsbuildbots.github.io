# App Development 📱
## Cadette Coding for Good Badge — Girls Build Bots
### Presented by your robotics mentors

---

# The capstone "for good" badge

Today you design an app for **social good**:

- A real community problem
- A real user
- A defensible solution

**The standard is professional:**
user research before design, design before code.

---

# Apps for good, deconstructed 🔍

Look at two real social-good apps — a food-rescue app,
a crisis-help finder. For each:

- Who's the **user**?
- What **problem**?
- **What's the smallest thing it does that helps?**

---

# Meet the MVP

**Minimum Viable Product.**

Real developers ship the **minimum that solves the problem**,
then grow it.

### Big hearts. Small first versions.

---

# User Research 🎤

Your team picks a community problem — a card, or better,
a real one you know.

A mentor plays the user. Run a real interview:

- "Walk me through the problem."
- "What have you tried?"
- "What would *solved* look like?"

---

# Rule: no design talk until research is done ✋

Sketching during the interview means you've stopped listening.

---

# Your deliverable 📝

### *[User] needs a way to [goal] because [barrier].*

Plus the **top two must-haves** — in the user's own words.

---

# User flow first 🗺️

Sticky-note map the user's path:

### open app → do the core thing → confirmation

**Three to five steps. No more.**

---

# Then wireframe 📐

1. One paper screen per sticky note
2. **The core action gets the biggest button**
3. **Accessibility pass** — big touch targets, readable text.
   Does it work for a user who is older, in a hurry, or new to phones?
4. **Feature-cut round** — anything not serving the problem statement goes

---

# Accessibility is the "for good," made concrete

### An app that excludes users isn't solving the community's problem.

**Cutting is designing.**

---

# Click Test 👆

The mentor-user thinks aloud while tapping your paper prototype.

**Your team stays silent.** Swap screens. Take notes.

Then make **one fix**.

---

# The Pitch — 90 seconds ⏱️

1. The **problem**
2. The **user**
3. Your **MVP**
4. What you'd **build next** with more time

---

# Optional: make it real 💻

In **MIT App Inventor** (appinventor.mit.edu):

Build the welcome screen + core button as a **running app**
on a phone or emulator.

### One working screen beats five imagined ones.

---

# Robot moment 🤖

We scope our robot each season to an **MVP** —
the minimum machine that scores — before adding stretch features.

Same discipline. Bigger build.
