# Mechanics make feelings

Designers **engineer emotion** on purpose:

| Mechanic | Feeling it manufactures |
|---|---|
| Countdown timer | tension |
| Growing combo counter | mastery |
| A near-miss | "one more try" |

---

# Dissect one you know 🔍

Pick a game this room plays.

- What's its **core mechanic**?
- What **feeling** does that mechanic manufacture?

---

# The ethics beat ⚖️

The same tools can **respect** players — or **hook** them unfairly.

### Designing *for good* means fun that leaves players glad they played.

That's a choice you make. Every time.

---

# Design doc — with a curve 📄

One page, before any code:

1. One **core mechanic**
2. **Goal**
3. **Fail state**
4. **Intended feeling**
5. A sketched **difficulty curve**

---

# The difficulty curve 📈

### How does minute three get harder than minute one?

- Faster enemies?
- Shorter timer?
- More obstacles?

**Scope rule:** one mechanic executed well beats four executed poorly.

---

# Build the prototype 💻

1. **Core loop** — player, objective, fail state
2. **Score** + win/lose conditions
3. **The difficulty curve, implemented** — a variable that ramps
   speed, spawn rate, or time pressure as score climbs
4. **Game feel pass** — sound effects, a screen shake or flash
5. Stretch: title screen + "play again" flow

---

# The game feel pass ✨

Tiny touches. Huge difference.

### This is the minute your prototype starts feeling like a real game.

---

# Structured playtest 🔬

**Not a swap. A protocol.**

**Half one — observe silently, 2 minutes:**
Where do they struggle? Smile? Quit?

**Half two — interview, 3 questions:**
1. What was the goal?
2. What frustrated you?
3. What would you change?

---

# Watch, don't explain 🤐

You will *ache* to explain your game mid-test.

Don't.

### What testers **do** outranks what they **say**.

---

# Iterate from the data 🔧

Implement **one change** from your protocol sheet.

**Closing:** name the observation that surprised you most.

*That surprise is why studios playtest.*
