# App Development 📱
## Junior Coding for Good Badge — Girls Build Bots
### Presented by your robotics mentors!

---

# Start with a USER 💚

App developers never start with the code.

They start with **a person and their problem**.

This is the purest "for good" badge in the series.

---

# Reverse-engineer an app 🔍

Look at an app you know. Ask the designer questions:

- **Who is the user?**
- **What problem does it solve?**
- **What's the very first thing you see — and why?**

Big buttons for important actions. The main thing front and center.

**Apps are designed, not accidental.**

---

# Interview your user 🎤

Your team draws a **user card**. A mentor plays that person.

Ask them:
- "Walk me through your day."
- "When does the problem happen?"
- "What have you tried?"

---

# Write the problem statement 📝

### *[User] needs a way to [goal] because [barrier].*

Developers who skip this step **build apps nobody needs**.

---

# Wireframe the app ✏️

Paper prototype, 4–5 screens:

1. **Welcome** — name + what it does, *in the user's words*
2. **Main screen** — the one button that solves the problem (make it BIG)
3. **Payoff** — what happens after the tap
4. **Extra feature** — *only if the interview justifies it*

---

# The developer's question 💭

As you design, we'll keep asking:

### "Which screen solves the **problem statement**?"

If a screen doesn't serve the user, **cut it**.
Real developers cut features constantly.

---

# Click Test 👆

Your user comes back!

1. The user taps your paper buttons
2. Your team swaps screens by hand
3. **Does the user solve their problem in three taps or fewer?**

Then make one fix based on what confused them.

---

# Robot moment 🤖

Our team designs the **driver dashboard** the same way.

The driver is the user. The interface shows exactly what
matters mid-match — **nothing more**.

---

# Present your app 🎤

One sentence:

### "*[App name]* helps *[user]* to *[solve problem]*."

---

# What you accomplished ✅

- Identified a real app's user and the problem it solves
- Interviewed a user and wrote a problem statement
- Designed a wireframe where every screen serves the user
- Ran a click test and improved from real feedback
