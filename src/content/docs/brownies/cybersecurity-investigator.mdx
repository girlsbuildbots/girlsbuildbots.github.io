## About this badge

**Badge theme:** Playing detective — spotting clues, thinking critically about what we see online, and investigating like cyber sleuths.

### The through-line: being safe and smart online

The Cybersecurity series builds digital street-smarts: understand how information travels (Basics), protect it (Safeguards), and **think critically to solve problems** (Investigator). Keep it concrete and friendly — never scary.

**Vocabulary:** investigate, clue, evidence, real vs. fake, trustworthy, think critically.

:::note[Official requirements]
The step-by-step requirements for the Brownie Cybersecurity Investigator badge are
published by Girl Scouts of the USA. Troop leaders access them free in the
[Volunteer Toolkit](https://mygs.girlscouts.org), or purchase the badge and requirement pamphlet
from the [Girl Scout Shop](https://www.girlscoutshop.com/badges-program/all-badges-pins/stem). Free sample activities for many
badges are on the [Girl Scout Activity Zone](https://www.girlscouts.org/en/activities-for-girls.html).

*Always verify workshop activities against the official steps before awarding the badge.*
:::

### Activity 1 — Detectives Look for Clues (15 min)

Cyber investigators are detectives — they look closely and ask questions before they trust something. Warm up with a quick "spot the difference" between a real thing and a fake, so Scouts practice noticing small details.

### Activity 2 — Real or Fake? (25 min)

Show message cards one at a time. For each, Scouts hunt for **clues**: Too-good-to-be-true prizes? Lots of !!!? Spelling mistakes? Asking for personal information? They vote real or fake and explain their clue. **The skill: don't trust automatically — investigate first.**

### Activity 3 — Crack the Case (25 min)

A friendly staged mystery: "Someone got a message saying they won a prize — should they click it?" In small teams, Scouts examine 2–3 clue cards (the message, a fake sender, a suspicious link drawn on paper) and decide together: safe or trick? Then present their verdict and the clue that gave it away.

### Activity 4 — Investigator Oath (5 min) + Wrap (5 min)

Each Scout finishes: "Before I trust something online, I will ___." (Look for clues / ask a grown-up / check if it's too good to be true.) Hand out detective badge stickers.

:::tip[Robot moment (optional)]
Share for 60 seconds how the team investigates when the robot misbehaves — looking for clues in the code to find the real problem, just like a cyber detective.
:::

### Materials

- Printed "message" cards: some real-looking and safe, some obvious fakes ("You won a FREE puppy, click here!!!")
- A simple "spot the difference" pair (a real-looking logo vs. a slightly-off fake)
- Magnifying glasses (optional but Brownies love them), detective "badge" stickers

## Completion checklist

By the end of the session, to earn the badge, each Scout can:

- [ ] Explain that a cyber investigator looks for clues before trusting something
- [ ] Spot clues that a message might be fake (too-good-to-be-true, misspellings, asking for private info)
- [ ] Work with a team to decide if something is safe or a trick
- [ ] Name one thing she'll do before trusting something online
