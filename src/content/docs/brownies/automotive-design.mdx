## About this badge

**Badge theme:** Thinking like a car designer — who the vehicle is for, what it needs to do, and how it should look — then sketching and modeling one.

### The through-line: designing for a driver

The Automotive series follows a car from idea to road: **Design** it, **engineer** it to move, then **manufacture** it. Across all three, Brownies learn that engineers build for a *person* — a driver with real needs. Mentors: keep asking "who is this car for?"

**Vocabulary:** design, vehicle, wheel, chassis (the frame), passenger, sketch, model.

:::note[Official requirements]
The step-by-step requirements for the Brownie Automotive Design badge are
published by Girl Scouts of the USA. Troop leaders access them free in the
[Volunteer Toolkit](https://mygs.girlscouts.org), or purchase the badge and requirement pamphlet
from the [Girl Scout Shop](https://www.girlscoutshop.com/badges-program/all-badges-pins/stem). Free sample activities for many
badges are on the [Girl Scout Activity Zone](https://www.girlscouts.org/en/activities-for-girls.html).

*Always verify workshop activities against the official steps before awarding the badge.*
:::

### Activity 1 — Cars Have a Job and a Driver (15 min)

Look at different vehicles (fire truck, ice-cream truck, race car, minivan). For each: **who drives it, and what does it need to do that job?** A fire truck needs a big ladder; an ice-cream truck needs a freezer. Cars are designed *around* their driver's needs.

### Activity 2 — Meet Your Customer (15 min)

Each Scout (or pair) draws a **customer card**. They fill the design sheet for that specific driver: who they are, what they need, and one special feature their car will have. Designing for a real customer — not just "a cool car" — is the heart of the badge.

### Activity 3 — Sketch, Then Build a Concept Model (40 min)

Designers sketch before they build. Scouts draw their car, then build a cardboard **concept model** with wheels that turn. It doesn't have to drive — it has to show the design and its special feature for that customer.

### Activity 4 — Design Show (10 min) + Wrap (5 min)

Each Scout shows her car and names the driver and the special feature in one sentence ("This is for a dog-walker, so it has a comfy pet seat!"). Gallery walk with one kind comment each.

:::tip[Robot moment (optional)]
Share for 60 seconds how the team designs its robot around the year's challenge — deciding what it needs to *do* before deciding what it looks like, just like designing a car for a driver.
:::

### Materials

- Cardboard, construction paper, bottle caps (wheels), craft sticks, tape, markers
- "Customer cards" (a farmer, a dog-walker, a pizza deliverer, a family with three kids)
- Design sheet: My driver / What they need / My car's special feature

## Completion checklist

By the end of the session, to earn the badge, each Scout can:

- [ ] Explain that vehicles are designed around a driver's needs
- [ ] Describe a specific customer and what their car needs to do
- [ ] Sketch a car design before building it
- [ ] Build a concept model with a special feature and explain who it's for
