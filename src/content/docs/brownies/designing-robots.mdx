## About this badge

This badge explores the jobs robots do for people and challenges Scouts to design a robot that solves an everyday problem. When she's earned it, a Brownie can explain sense–think–act and has designed and built her own robot prototype.

**Scouts will learn:**

- The many jobs robots do — at home, in space, in factories, in hospitals
- Sense → Think → Act, acted out with their own bodies
- The engineer's three questions: what does it sense, think about, and do?
- Prototyping: a first model shows the idea — it doesn't have to work

:::note[Official requirements]
The step-by-step requirements for the Brownie Designing Robots badge are
published by Girl Scouts of the USA. Troop leaders access them free in the
[Volunteer Toolkit](https://mygs.girlscouts.org), or purchase the badge and requirement pamphlet
from the [Girl Scout Shop](https://www.girlscoutshop.com/badges-program/all-badges-pins/stem). Free sample activities for many
badges are on the [Girl Scout Activity Zone](https://www.girlscouts.org/en/activities-for-girls.html).

*Always verify workshop activities against the official steps before awarding the badge.*
:::

## Meeting plan

<p class="meeting-chips">
  <Badge text="⏱ 90 minutes" variant="tip" size="medium" />
  <Badge text="👥 1 mentor : 3–4 Scouts" variant="tip" size="medium" />

- Robot jobs card sort (15 min)
- Sense–Think–Act movement game (15 min)
- Build a craft prototype + design sheet (45 min)
- Gallery walk with sticky-note compliments (15 min)

**Materials:** Craft supplies (cardboard, cups, pipe cleaners, googly eyes, foil, straws), robot-job picture cards, design sheets.

## Completion checklist

By the end of the session, each Scout:

- [ ] Can name jobs robots do for people
- [ ] Can explain sense, think, act in her own words
- [ ] Designed a robot that solves an everyday problem
- [ ] Built a prototype and completed a design sheet
