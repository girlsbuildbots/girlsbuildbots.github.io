## About this badge

**Badge theme:** Engineering a vehicle that actually moves — building it, testing it on a track, and improving the design based on what happens.

### The through-line: designing for a driver

The Automotive series follows a car from idea to road: design it, **engineer** it to move, then manufacture it. Across all three, Brownies learn that engineers build for a *person* — and that engineers **test and improve** their work.

**Vocabulary:** engineer, force, ramp, test, improve, friction (grippy vs. slippery), fair test.

:::note[Official requirements]
The step-by-step requirements for the Brownie Automotive Engineering badge are

### Activity 1 — What Makes a Car Go? (15 min)

No engine today — we use a ramp and **gravity**. Talk about **force** (a push or pull) and **friction** (grippy slows you down, slippery lets you roll). Quick prediction: will a heavier car or a lighter car roll farther? Everyone guesses — engineers predict before they test.

### Activity 2 — Build Your Roller (25 min)

Scouts build a simple cardboard car with axles and wheels that spin freely. Mentors help troubleshoot wobbly wheels (a real engineering problem!). Goal: it rolls straight down the ramp.

### Activity 3 — Test, Change, Test Again (30 min)

The engineering heart of the session. Each car rolls down the ramp; Scouts measure how far it goes and record it. Then change **one thing** — add weight, swap wheels, straighten the axle — and test again. Did it help? **The rule: change only one thing at a time**, or you won't know what worked. That's a *fair test*.

### Activity 4 — Best Improvement Wins (5 min) + Wrap (5 min)

Not "whose car is fastest," but "who made the biggest *improvement*." Each Scout shares one change she made and what happened. Celebrate the engineering, not just the winner.

:::tip[Robot moment (optional)]
Share for 60 seconds how the team tests the robot over and over, changing one thing at a time, to make it work better — the same fair-test habit.
:::

### Materials

- Cardboard, bottle caps or plastic lids (wheels), skewers or straws (axles), tape
- A ramp (a board or stiff cardboard propped on books/chairs)
- Tape measure or a floor marked with tape lines; sticky notes for recording distances

## Completion checklist

By the end of the session, to earn the badge, each Scout can:

- [ ] Explain that a force (like gravity on a ramp) makes a car move
- [ ] Build a car with wheels that roll
- [ ] Test her car and measure how it did
- [ ] Change one thing, test again, and say whether it improved
