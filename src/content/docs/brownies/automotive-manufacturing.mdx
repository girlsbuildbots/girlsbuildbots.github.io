## About this badge

**Badge theme:** Discovering how cars get made — assembly lines, teamwork, and quality — by manufacturing something together, step by step.

### The through-line: designing for a driver

The Automotive series follows a car from idea to road: design it, engineer it to move, then **manufacture** it. This final badge asks: once you have one great car, how do you make *hundreds* — the same, fast, and well-made?

**Vocabulary:** manufacture, factory, assembly line, station, teamwork, quality, inspector.

:::note[Official requirements]
The step-by-step requirements for the Brownie Automotive Manufacturing badge are
published by Girl Scouts of the USA. Troop leaders access them free in the

### Activity 1 — One Car vs. a Hundred Cars (10 min)

It's one thing to build a single model — but a real car company makes thousands, all alike. How? **The assembly line:** instead of one person building a whole car, each person does one job as the car moves past. Henry Ford made this famous.

### Activity 2 — Round 1: Everyone Solo (15 min)

Each Scout makes one complete paper car by herself, doing every step. Time it. Notice: it's slow, and the cars come out a little different from each other.

### Activity 3 — Round 2: Build the Assembly Line (35 min)

Now set up stations — fold, wheels, windows, decorate, **inspect** — one Scout (or pair) per station. The cars move down the line; each Scout does only her job, over and over. One Scout is the **quality inspector** with a checklist, sending back any car that's missing a part. Time the line. Compare: faster? more consistent? What happened when a station got backed up?

### Activity 4 — Factory Debrief (5 min) + Wrap (5 min)

Talk it through: the line made more cars, more alike, faster — but everyone had to work as a team, and one slow station slowed everybody. That's real manufacturing. Count the finished cars; celebrate the team's output.

:::tip[Robot moment (optional)]
Share for 60 seconds how the team divides its build season into roles and stations — programming, building, wiring — so lots of work happens at once, just like a factory line.
:::

### Materials

- Paper-car templates (a simple fold-and-decorate car, identical for everyone)
- Craft supplies split into stations: fold, add wheels (stickers/stamps), add windows, decorate, inspect
- Two timers (phone stopwatches are fine); a "quality checklist" card for the inspector

## Completion checklist

By the end of the session, to earn the badge, each Scout can:

- [ ] Explain what an assembly line is and why factories use one
- [ ] Work one station as part of a manufacturing team
- [ ] Compare building solo vs. building on a line (speed and consistency)
- [ ] Describe why quality checking matters in manufacturing
