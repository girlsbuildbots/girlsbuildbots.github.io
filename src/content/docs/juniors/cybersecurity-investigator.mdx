## About this badge

**Badge theme:** How cyber cases get cracked — following digital trails, evaluating evidence, and reaching conclusions you can defend.

### The through-line: being safe and smart online

The series builds real digital literacy: understand how information travels (Basics), protect it (Safeguards), and **investigate critically** (Investigator). Juniors can follow evidence trails and reason from clues — real investigative thinking.

**Vocabulary:** evidence, clue, trail, verify, source, timeline, conclusion.

:::note[Official requirements]
The step-by-step requirements for the Junior Cybersecurity Investigator badge are
published by Girl Scouts of the USA. Troop leaders access them free in the
[Volunteer Toolkit](https://mygs.girlscouts.org), or purchase the badge and requirement pamphlet
from the [Girl Scout Shop](https://www.girlscoutshop.com/badges-program/all-badges-pins/stem). Free sample activities for many
badges are on the [Girl Scout Activity Zone](https://www.girlscouts.org/en/activities-for-girls.html).

*Always verify workshop activities against the official steps before awarding the badge.*
:::

### Activity 1 — How Investigators Think (15 min)

Investigators don't guess — they collect **evidence**, check whether each **source** is trustworthy, and build a **timeline** before reaching a conclusion. Quick warm-up: show one suspicious message; as a group, list every observable clue before deciding anything. Observation first, verdict last.

### Activity 2 — The Case of the Prize Message (45 min)

The main event, in teams of 3–4. Each team receives the fictional case file: a classmate (invented) got a message saying she won a tablet — is it real? Teams work the evidence:
1. **Log each clue** on the evidence sheet (misspelled sender, urgent tone, link text doesn't match the label, timeline shows the "company" account was created yesterday)
2. **Verify** using station cards — what would checking the real company's site show? What would asking the sender directly reveal?
3. **Build the timeline** of what happened when
4. **Reach a conclusion** with the evidence that supports it

Mentors play witnesses/resources when teams "verify," feeding answers from a prepared key.

### Activity 3 — Present the Case (20 min)

Each team presents like investigators: the evidence, the verification steps, the conclusion, and their recommendation ("don't click; report it; here's why"). Other teams may cross-examine with one question. The bar: **could you defend your conclusion with evidence alone?**

### Activity 4 — Investigator's Habits (10 min) + Wrap (5 min)

Distill the method into three habits Scouts keep: look before trusting, verify with a second source, and conclusions need evidence. Closing: each Scout names the clue from today she'll spot fastest next time.

:::tip[Robot moment (optional)]
Share for 60 seconds how the team investigates a robot failure the same way — logging symptoms, checking one suspect at a time, and only "fixing" what the evidence points to.
:::

### Materials

- A staged case file per team (mentor-prepared, entirely fictional): a printed suspicious message, a "sender profile" card, a hand-drawn "link preview," and a simple event timeline
- Evidence log sheets: Clue / What it tells us / Trustworthy?
- "Verify it" station cards (check the sender, check the link spelling, check with the person it claims to be from)
- Detective props if you like — the framing works

## Completion checklist

By the end of the session, to earn the badge, each Scout can:

- [ ] Explain how investigators use evidence, sources, and timelines
- [ ] Log clues from a suspicious message and rate their trustworthiness
- [ ] Verify a claim using a second source
- [ ] Present a conclusion supported by evidence
