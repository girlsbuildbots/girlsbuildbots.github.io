## About this badge

Juniors investigate the solar system — planets, light and shadow, and the tools astronomers use to study objects millions of miles away.

:::note[Official requirements]
The step-by-step requirements for the Junior Space Science Investigator badge are
published by Girl Scouts of the USA. Troop leaders access them free in the
[Volunteer Toolkit](https://mygs.girlscouts.org), or purchase the badge and requirement pamphlet
from the [Girl Scout Shop](https://www.girlscoutshop.com/badges-program/all-badges-pins/stem). Free sample activities for many
badges are on the [Girl Scout Activity Zone](https://www.girlscouts.org/en/activities-for-girls.html).

*Always verify workshop activities against the official steps before awarding the badge.*
:::

## Leading the meeting

Build a scale solar system down a hallway or field (the distances shock everyone), run light-and-shadow experiments, and end with Stellarium on the projector to 'fly' through tonight's sky. Tie-in: Mars rovers are robot astronauts.

## Best free resources

- [NASA Space Place — space science for kids](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov)
- [Stellarium Web — free planetarium in the browser](https://stellarium-web.org)
- [NASA STEM — activities & career features](https://www.nasa.gov/stem)

Planning help — mentor ratios, timing, surveys, and a 6-week checklist — is in the
[Resource Guide](/downloads/resource-guide.md).
