## About this badge

This badge covers how robots are built and challenges Juniors to design and build a robot model that solves a problem. When she's earned it, a Junior knows a robot's five part groups and has taken a design from problem to improved prototype.

**Scouts will learn:**

- Robot anatomy: sensors, controller, chassis, motors, effectors
- The engineering design process, end to end
- Designing for a real person's real problem
- Testing, feedback, and making one documented improvement

:::note[Official requirements]
The step-by-step requirements for the Junior Designing Robots badge are
published by Girl Scouts of the USA. Troop leaders access them free in the
[Volunteer Toolkit](https://mygs.girlscouts.org), or purchase the badge and requirement pamphlet
from the [Girl Scout Shop](https://www.girlscoutshop.com/badges-program/all-badges-pins/stem). Free sample activities for many
badges are on the [Girl Scout Activity Zone](https://www.girlscouts.org/en/activities-for-girls.html).

*Always verify workshop activities against the official steps before awarding the badge.*
:::

## Meeting plan

<p class="meeting-chips">
  <Badge text="⏱ 2 hours" variant="tip" size="medium" />
  <Badge text="👥 1 mentor : 4–5 Scouts" variant="tip" size="medium" />
  <Badge text="💻 MakeCode Arcade — free" variant="tip" size="medium" />
</p>

- Robot Anatomy teardown + quiz game (20 min)
- Design Sprint in teams: define → plan → build → test → improve (70 min)
- Design Review Circle with 'two stars and a wish' feedback (30 min)

**Materials:** Parts-diagram poster, planning sheets, build supplies (cardboard, craft materials, LEGO/K'NEX if available), mentor-run hot glue station.

## Completion checklist

By the end of the session, each Scout:

- [ ] Can name a robot's main part groups and their jobs
- [ ] Followed all steps of the engineering design process
- [ ] Built a labeled robot model that addresses a real problem
- [ ] Made at least one improvement from testing or feedback
