## About this badge

Juniors peek behind the curtain of entertainment — the science and technology inside animation, special effects, video games, and amusement park rides.

:::note[Official requirements]
The step-by-step requirements for the Junior Entertainment Technology badge are
published by Girl Scouts of the USA. Troop leaders access them free in the
[Volunteer Toolkit](https://mygs.girlscouts.org), or purchase the badge and requirement pamphlet
from the [Girl Scout Shop](https://www.girlscoutshop.com/badges-program/all-badges-pins/stem). Free sample activities for many
badges are on the [Girl Scout Activity Zone](https://www.girlscouts.org/en/activities-for-girls.html).

*Always verify workshop activities against the official steps before awarding the badge.*
:::

## Leading the meeting

Three-station rotation: make a flipbook or stop-motion clip (animation), explore Pixar in a Box (real studio tech), and design a paper roller coaster (ride physics). Mentors demo how game controllers and robot controllers are cousins.

## Best free resources

- [Pixar in a Box (Khan Academy) — the tech behind animation](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar)
- [Scratch — free coding & game design (MIT)](https://scratch.mit.edu)
- [TeachEngineering — free K–12 engineering lessons](https://www.teachengineering.org)

Planning help — mentor ratios, timing, surveys, and a 6-week checklist — is in the
[Resource Guide](/downloads/resource-guide.md).
