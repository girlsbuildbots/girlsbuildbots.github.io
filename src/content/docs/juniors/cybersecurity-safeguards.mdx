## About this badge

**Badge theme:** Practical protections — strong passwords, privacy awareness, digital footprints, and spotting phishing red flags.

### The through-line: being safe and smart online

The series builds real digital literacy: understand how information travels (Basics), **protect it** (Safeguards), and investigate critically (Investigator). Juniors can learn concrete, practical protections they'll actually use.

**Vocabulary:** password, passphrase, phishing, red flag, privacy setting, digital footprint, two-factor (a second lock).

:::note[Official requirements]
The step-by-step requirements for the Junior Cybersecurity Safeguards badge are
published by Girl Scouts of the USA. Troop leaders access them free in the
[Volunteer Toolkit](https://mygs.girlscouts.org), or purchase the badge and requirement pamphlet
from the [Girl Scout Shop](https://www.girlscoutshop.com/badges-program/all-badges-pins/stem). Free sample activities for many
badges are on the [Girl Scout Activity Zone](https://www.girlscouts.org/en/activities-for-girls.html).

*Always verify workshop activities against the official steps before awarding the badge.*
:::

### Activity 1 — Password Strength Lab (25 min)

Sort made-up passwords weakest to strongest. Discover the rules by pattern: **longer beats clever**, mixed characters help, and a **passphrase** (three random words: "PurpleRobotTaco!") is both strong and memorable. Each Scout invents a made-up passphrase using the method — and does NOT write down or share any real one. Mention the second lock: some accounts add a code sent to a grown-up's phone (**two-factor**) — two locks beat one.

### Activity 2 — Spot the Phish (30 min)

Deal the mentor-made "inbox." For each message, teams hunt **red flags**: urgent pressure ("act NOW!"), prizes for nothing, misspelled sender addresses, links that don't match, requests for personal info or passwords. Score a point per correctly-sorted message, with the rule of thumb: **real companies don't ask for your password in a message.** When unsure — don't click; ask a trusted adult.

### Activity 3 — Footprints in the Sand (20 min)

Scenario cards: for each action (post a photo, take a fun quiz, share a location), ask — who can see this? How long does it last? Would you want it visible in five years? Juniors grasp the honest version: things shared online can persist and travel. The safeguard: **think before you post, and use privacy settings with a grown-up's help.**

### Activity 4 — My Safeguard Kit (10 min) + Wrap (5 min)

Each Scout writes her personal top-three safeguards (a passphrase method, a phishing red flag she'll watch for, a think-before-posting rule). Closing: pairs quiz each other with one made-up message — phish or legit?

:::tip[Robot moment (optional)]
Share for 60 seconds how the team protects its accounts and code — strong passphrases and never sharing logins — because losing control of the robot's code would be a very bad day.
:::

### Materials

- **Made-up** password examples for the strength lab (never real passwords — say this rule out loud)
- Mentor-made "inbox" of printed example messages: some legit, some phishing (fake prize, fake "your account is locked," a too-good-to-be-true offer)
- "Digital footprint" scenario cards (a photo posted, a comment made, a quiz taken, a location shared)
- Optional: Interland "Tower of Treasure" for reinforcement

## Completion checklist

By the end of the session, to earn the badge, each Scout can:

- [ ] Explain what makes a password strong and build a made-up passphrase
- [ ] Spot at least three phishing red flags in example messages
- [ ] Explain what a digital footprint is and why it lasts
- [ ] Name her personal top-three safeguards
