## About this badge

Juniors investigate wild animals and the habitats they depend on — how habitats meet animal needs, why they're threatened, and how people help protect them.

:::note[Official requirements]
The step-by-step requirements for the Junior Animal Habitats badge are
published by Girl Scouts of the USA. Troop leaders access them free in the
[Volunteer Toolkit](https://mygs.girlscouts.org), or purchase the badge and requirement pamphlet
from the [Girl Scout Shop](https://www.girlscoutshop.com/badges-program/all-badges-pins/stem). Free sample activities for many
badges are on the [Girl Scout Activity Zone](https://www.girlscouts.org/en/activities-for-girls.html).

*Always verify workshop activities against the official steps before awarding the badge.*
:::

## Leading the meeting

Each team researches one habitat and builds a shoebox diorama with its animals' needs labeled (food, water, shelter, space). The tech tie-in: wildlife-monitoring robots, camera traps, and conservation drones.

## Best free resources

- [National Geographic Kids — animals & nature](https://kids.nationalgeographic.com)
- [Seek by iNaturalist — free species-ID app](https://www.inaturalist.org/pages/seek_app)

Planning help — mentor ratios, timing, surveys, and a 6-week checklist — is in the
[Resource Guide](/downloads/resource-guide.md).
