## About this badge

**Badge theme:** How assembly lines, quality control, and teamwork turn one design into many identical vehicles — and how factories keep improving.

### The through-line: from idea to road

Design it, engineer it, then **manufacture it at scale** — thousands of identical, safe vehicles. Juniors are ready for the real concepts: assembly lines, quality control, bottlenecks, and continuous improvement.

**Vocabulary:** assembly line, station, quality control, defect, bottleneck, throughput, continuous improvement.

### Activity 1 — The Scale Problem (10 min)

One great prototype isn't a car company — factories make *thousands*, identical and safe. How? Assembly lines: each worker masters one step as the product flows past. Today the troop becomes a factory, and we'll measure it like one.

### Activity 2 — Round 1: Craft Production (Solo Builds) (15 min)

Everyone builds one complete car alone, all steps, 8 minutes. Tally: how many finished? Line them up — how identical are they? Note the honest results on the board (slow, and every car's a little different — that's craft production).

### Activity 3 — Round 2: The Assembly Line (30 min)

Set up stations with one Scout (or pair) per step, plus a **Quality Control inspector** with the spec card who rejects cars missing anything (rejects go back to the failing station — defects are information, not shame). Run 10 minutes. Tally throughput and defects. Almost always: more cars, more consistent — and one station drowning in a pile. Name it: a **bottleneck**.

### Activity 4 — Kaizen Round: Improve the Line (25 min)

Real factories never run the same line twice — they improve it (**continuous improvement**). Team huddle: where was the bottleneck? What would fix it — move a person, split a step, pre-stage supplies? Apply the top ideas and run 10 more minutes. Compare all three rounds on the tally board: solo vs. line vs. improved line. The improvement round almost always wins big — and the *Scouts* engineered that gain.

### Activity 5 — Debrief (10 min) + Wrap (5 min)

Three questions: Why was the line faster? Why did QC matter (would you ride in a car nobody inspected)? What did your improvement change? Count the total fleet and celebrate the team's output — manufacturing is a team sport.

:::tip[Robot moment (optional)]
Share for 60 seconds how build season runs like this — parallel stations (wiring, programming, machining), inspections before competition, and constant process improvements between events.
:::

### Materials

- A standard origami/paper car design with 6–7 distinct build steps (fold, wheels, windows, stripe, headlights, final check)
- Station signs + supplies pre-sorted per station; a **spec card** at Quality Control showing exactly what a "passing" car looks like
- Two timers; a production tally board (made / passed QC / defects)
- Sticky notes for the improvement round

## Completion checklist

By the end of the session, to earn the badge, each Scout can:

- [ ] Explain why factories use assembly lines instead of solo craft builds
- [ ] Work a station and describe how quality control catches defects
- [ ] Identify a bottleneck from production data
- [ ] Propose and test an improvement, and compare results across rounds
