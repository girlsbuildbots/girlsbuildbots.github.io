## About this badge

### The big idea

Cadettes are close enough to high school that career exploration gets practical: what do STEM jobs *actually involve* day to day, what's the pathway (classes, clubs, degrees), and what can a middle schooler do *this year* that counts? The team's honest answer — "we're on that pathway right now, here's what it looks like" — is the badge's superpower.

**Vocabulary:** career pathway, day-in-the-life, internship, portfolio, mentor, informational interview.

:::note[Official requirements]
The step-by-step requirements for the Cadette STEM Career Exploration badge are
published by Girl Scouts of the USA. Troop leaders access them free in the
[Volunteer Toolkit](https://mygs.girlscouts.org), or purchase the badge and requirement pamphlet
from the [Girl Scout Shop](https://www.girlscoutshop.com/badges-program/all-badges-pins/stem). Free sample activities for many
badges are on the [Girl Scout Activity Zone](https://www.girlscouts.org/en/activities-for-girls.html).

*Always verify workshop activities against the official steps before awarding the badge.*
:::

### Activity 1 — Myth vs. Day-in-the-Life (20 min)

Deal the career cards. Teams read and report the surprise: what does this job *actually* involve vs. the TV version? (Software engineers spend more time reading code than writing it; scientists spend a lot of time writing.) Point: choosing a career means knowing its Tuesdays, not its highlight reel.

### Activity 2 — The Panel, Cadette Edition (25 min)

Team members present 3-minute versions of their story with the practical layer Cadettes want: what classes they chose and why, how they joined the team, what they're aiming for after high school, and one honest struggle. Center the young women on the team. Then open Q&A — Cadettes ask sharper questions than anyone expects; let them.

### Activity 3 — Informational Interviews (35 min)

The badge's core skill, done for real: in pairs, Cadettes conduct 8-minute **informational interviews** with team members using the question sheet (What does your role involve? What surprised you? What would you tell someone my age? What's the next step on your path?), taking notes, then rotating once. Interviewing a person about their path is a career skill they'll use for decades — say so.

### Activity 4 — Pathway Map + Commitment (25 min) + Wrap (15 min)

Each Cadette builds her **pathway map**: a career direction that interests her → what she can do *now* (a club, a free course, FLL/FTC, a science fair) → high school moves (classes, teams) → after. The map is a draft, not a contract — paths change, and the panel just proved it. Closing round: each Cadette shares her now-step and *when* she'll take it. Optional robot demo finale — hands on the controls.

:::tip[Robot moment (optional)]
Let Cadettes drive the robot, then land the line: "Every person who built this started with a step exactly the size of the one on your map."
:::

### Materials

- "Day in the life" career cards, each with the honest version: a software engineer (mostly reading and fixing code, meetings, teamwork), a biomedical engineer, a data scientist, a robotics technician, a UX designer, an environmental engineer — real tasks, typical pathway, and one myth busted per card
- Informational-interview question sheets; pathway-map worksheets (now → high school → after)
- Team members prepped for panel + interviews; optional robot for demos

## Completion checklist

By the end of the session, to earn the badge, each Scout can:

- [ ] Describe the realistic day-to-day of at least two STEM careers
- [ ] Conduct an informational interview and record what she learned
- [ ] Map a pathway from a career interest back to actions she can take now
- [ ] Commit to one concrete next step with a timeframe
