## About this badge

**Badge theme:** Real protection strategies — authentication, social-engineering defense, and deliberately securing your digital life.

### The through-line: understanding, protecting, investigating

At the Cadette level, Safeguards gets real: attackers mostly don't hack computers — they hack *people*. Social engineering, urgency, impersonation. The defense is a calm, skeptical mind plus a few strong technical habits.

**Vocabulary:** social engineering, phishing, impersonation, authentication, two-factor (2FA), password manager, privacy audit.

### Activity 1 — Two Locks Beat One (Authentication Lab) (25 min)

Quick passphrase refresher (length beats cleverness; three-random-words method) using invented examples only — then level up to **authentication**: how does a system know you're you? Something you *know* (passphrase), something you *have* (a code on a phone — **2FA**), something you *are* (fingerprint). Demo the logic: a stolen passphrase alone fails against 2FA. Mention **password managers** as the grown-up tool: one strong master passphrase, unique passwords everywhere else. Each Scout leaves with a written personal authentication plan (made-up examples, real methods).

### Activity 2 — Social Engineering Defense (40 min)

The heart of the session. Work the scenario deck in teams: for each message, identify the **manipulation lever** — urgency ("in the next 10 minutes!"), authority ("this is the school office"), emotion ("your friend is stranded"), reward ("you've been selected"). Then the counter-move: **slow down, verify through a separate channel** (call the real number, ask the real friend in person), never act on pressure. The deck's planted legit message matters: the skill is discrimination, not blanket paranoia. Score: which team correctly sorts the full deck *and* names each lever?

### Activity 3 — Privacy Audit Workshop (30 min)

Each Cadette works the audit checklist against her own digital life *from memory* (no logging in at the workshop): what accounts exist, what does each know, who can see what, what would she change? Deliverable: a top-three action list to complete at home **with a parent** — tighten one setting, enable 2FA somewhere, retire an unused account. Frame it as taking inventory of your own house keys.

### Activity 4 — Defense Drill (15 min) + Wrap (10 min)

Rapid-fire: mentors read scenarios; teams call the lever and the counter-move. Closing: each Scout names the manipulation lever she thinks would most likely have worked on her *before* today — honest self-awareness is the strongest safeguard in the room.

:::tip[Robot moment (optional)]
Share for 60 seconds how the team protects accounts and code with unique passphrases and 2FA — and why "someone urgently needs the team login" is a message you verify, never obey.
:::

### Materials

- **Made-up** credential examples for the authentication lab (never real passwords — state this rule aloud)
- Mentor-made "social engineering" scenario deck: printed DMs/emails/texts with escalating subtlety (fake giveaway → fake friend-in-trouble → fake "school office" request → one perfectly legit message mixed in)
- "Privacy audit" checklist worksheet (account types, what each knows about you, sharing settings to review with a parent)
- Optional: NOVA Cybersecurity Lab for reinforcement

## Completion checklist

By the end of the session, to earn the badge, each Scout can:

- [ ] Explain authentication factors and why 2FA defeats a stolen password
- [ ] Identify the manipulation lever in a social-engineering attempt
- [ ] Apply the verify-through-another-channel counter-move
- [ ] Complete a privacy audit and commit to three concrete improvements
