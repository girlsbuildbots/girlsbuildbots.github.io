# The capstone

Today you run a **full incident investigation** — thinking like both:

- The **attacker** — how was this done?
- The **defender** — how do we prove it, and prevent it?

### The discipline: no conclusion outruns its support.

---

# How real investigations work

1. **Secure** the evidence
2. Build a **timeline**
3. Form **hypotheses**
4. **Test** each against the evidence
5. *Only then* conclude

---

# Two rules for today 📜

### 1. Every claim needs an exhibit.

### 2. The goal is the **attack vector** — how it happened — not just a culprit.

*And fair warning: this file contains a red herring.
Real cases always do.* 🙂

---

# The Case 📁

## "The Bake Sale Account Takeover"

A club's social account posted spam overnight.

**Your team gets the full file. Work it.**

---

# Step 1 — Log every exhibit 📝

| Exhibit | What it shows | Corroborated by | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | | high / med / low |

**Confidence matters as much as the finding.**

---

# Step 2 — Build the timeline 🕐

Lay the strips out physically:

### DM → click → fake login → 2:14 AM access → spam posts

---

# Step 3 — Form hypotheses 🔬

**At least two** — including the suspicious rival club member.

For each one:
- Which exhibits **support** it?
- Which exhibits **contradict** it?

---

# Step 4 — Name the attack vector 🎯

### phishing link → credential theft → account takeover

…with the **evidence chain** that proves it.

---

# When the red herring collapses 🐟

Someone "seemed suspicious" — but the evidence doesn't hold.

### Suspicion isn't proof.

That collapse is the most important moment of the session.

---

# The Incident Report 📄

Like professionals:

1. **What happened** — the timeline
2. **How** — attack vector + evidence chain
3. **What we can't prove** — state unknowns honestly.
   Real reports always have them.
4. **Remediation** — three defenses that would have stopped this

---

# Remediation ties the series together 🔗

- **Basics** explained the system
- **Safeguards** taught the defenses
- **Investigator** shows exactly where they'd have worked

*2FA · a URL-checking habit · verify before clicking*

---

# Case Presentations ⚖️

Five minutes per team. Then **one cross-examination question**:

> "How do you know the login wasn't the treasurer traveling?"

**Answer from exhibits.**
