# How cyber cases get cracked 🔍

Investigators **don't guess**. They:

1. Collect **evidence**
2. Check whether each **source** is trustworthy
3. Build a **timeline**
4. *Then* reach a conclusion

### Observation first. Verdict last.

---

# Warm-up 🧐

Here's one suspicious message.

**Before anyone decides anything** — list every clue you can observe.

*Every single one. We'll decide after.*

---

# The Case of the Prize Message 📁

**The case:** a classmate got a message saying she won a tablet.

### Is it real?

Your team gets the case file. Work the evidence.

---

# Step 1 — Log every clue 📝

On your evidence sheet: **Clue / What it tells us / Trustworthy?**

Look for:
- Misspelled sender
- Urgent tone
- Link text that doesn't match the label
- A timeline showing the "company" account was created **yesterday**

---

# Step 2 — Verify 🔎

Go to the verification stations:

- What would checking the **real company's site** show?
- What would **asking the sender directly** reveal?

**A second source is what turns a hunch into evidence.**

---

# Step 3 — Timeline, then conclusion 🕐

1. Build the **timeline** — what happened, and when?
2. Reach a **conclusion**
3. List the **evidence that supports it**

---

# Present the Case ⚖️

Like real investigators:

1. The **evidence**
2. The **verification steps**
3. The **conclusion**
4. Your **recommendation** — "don't click; report it; here's why"

Other teams get **one cross-examination question**.

---

# The bar 🎯

### Could you defend your conclusion with **evidence alone**?

Not "it felt fishy." **Evidence.**

---

# Robot moment 🤖

We investigate a robot failure exactly this way:

- Log the symptoms
- Check **one suspect at a time**
- Only "fix" what the evidence points to

---

# The Investigator's three habits 📜

1. **Look before trusting**
2. **Verify with a second source**
3. **Conclusions need evidence**

**Closing:** which clue from today will you spot fastest next time?

---
